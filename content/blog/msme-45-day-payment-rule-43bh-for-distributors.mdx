Since 1 April 2024, a business that buys from a micro or small enterprise and pays late can lose the tax deduction for that purchase in that year. For a manufacturer who sells to distributors on credit, that is the most useful change to payment discipline in a decade — if you know how it works. For a distributor who buys from MSME suppliers, it is a year-end risk to manage.

This is an explanation, not tax advice. Check your own position with your CA.

## The rule in one paragraph

Section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act says: an amount payable to a **micro or small enterprise** for goods or services is deductible only in the year it is **actually paid**, if it was not paid within the time allowed by **section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006**. If you pay within the time allowed, nothing changes — you claim the expense when you book it, as always.

## How long is "the time allowed"?

- **There is a written agreement on credit period:** the agreed period, but never more than **45 days**. Agree 30 days and the limit is 30. Agree 60 days and the limit is still 45.
- **No written agreement:** **15 days**.

Days are counted from the **day of acceptance** — normally the day the goods are delivered or the service is rendered. If the buyer objects in writing within 15 days of delivery, the clock starts when the supplier removes the objection.

## Who counts as a supplier here

- **Micro:** investment in plant and machinery up to ₹1 crore *and* turnover up to ₹5 crore.
- **Small:** investment up to ₹10 crore *and* turnover up to ₹50 crore.
- **Medium** enterprises are **not** covered by 43B(h).
- The supplier should hold **Udyam registration**. Ask for the certificate and keep it.
- **Traders:** wholesale and retail traders can register on Udyam, but the registration was allowed for priority-sector lending. The widely held view is that the delayed-payment provisions — and so 43B(h) — do not extend to purchases from pure traders. This is exactly the point to confirm with your CA.

The **buyer** can be any size. A large company buying from a small one is covered.

## What actually gets disallowed

Only what is unpaid, beyond its time limit, **on 31 March**.

- Invoice 10 January, 45-day terms, paid 20 March (late, but in the same year): fully deductible this year. You may owe interest, but there is no disallowance.
- Invoice 10 January, paid 15 April: not deductible this year. It becomes deductible next year, when it was paid.
- Invoice 1 March, 45-day terms: the limit runs to mid-April, so it is not yet late on 31 March. No disallowance, provided you pay in time.

Unlike other parts of section 43B, paying before the return-filing date does **not** rescue the deduction.

## The cost

A ₹10 lakh purchase disallowed adds ₹10 lakh to this year's taxable profit — roughly ₹2.5 to ₹3 lakh of tax paid a year early, depending on your rate. You get the deduction back the following year, so it is a cash-flow cost, not a permanent one. Separately, the MSMED Act charges compound interest at three times the bank rate on late payments, and that interest is never deductible.

## If you are the supplier

1. **Put your Udyam number on every invoice** and on your statement of account.
2. **Put the credit period in writing** — on the order confirmation or the invoice. No written terms means 15 days, which helps you, but written terms avoid arguments.
3. **Remind before 31 March, not after.** In February, send each customer a statement showing which bills will be past their limit at year-end. Your customer's accountant will thank you; this is a compliance favour, not pressure.
4. **Keep it polite.** The message is "this helps you keep your deduction", never a threat. There is wording you can borrow in [payment reminder messages that work](/blog/polite-payment-reminder-messages-english-hindi).

## If you are the buyer

1. Tag suppliers as micro, small, medium or other in your books, with their Udyam number.
2. From January, review payables to micro and small suppliers by due date under this rule, not just by your usual cycle.
3. Clear anything that will be over its limit before 31 March.

Rules that reward paying on time are rare. This one is worth understanding properly.
