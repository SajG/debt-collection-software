The outstanding report lives on the accountant's PC. The people who need it — you at a customer's shop, your salesperson before asking for the next order — are somewhere else. Here are the four ways to close that gap, from simplest to most useful.

## First, the report itself

In Tally Prime: **Gateway of Tally → Display More Reports → Statements of Accounts → Outstandings → Receivables**. Press `F6` for the age-wise view and set your buckets (0–30, 31–60, 61–90, over 90 days). For one customer, choose **Outstandings → Ledger** instead.

If the report shows only a total per customer and no bills, bill-wise details are not switched on. See [bill-wise outstanding in Tally explained](/blog/bill-wise-outstanding-in-tally-explained).

## 1. Export to PDF and send it on WhatsApp

`Alt+E` → **Export** → PDF. Send the file to the group.

It works today and costs nothing. But it is out of date by the evening, a 40-page PDF is hard to search on a phone, and every salesperson sees every customer — including ones that are not theirs.

## 2. Export to Excel and keep it on Google Drive

Export as Excel, upload, share. Now people can search and filter.

The problem is the same one in a different file: somebody has to export it every day, and nobody does after the second week. If you go this way, a clean layout helps — our free [outstanding ageing template](/tools/outstanding-ageing-template) works out the days overdue and the buckets for you.

## 3. Tally's own remote access

Tally offers browser access to reports with a TallyPrime subscription. It is live and it is from the source.

What you get is Tally's report in a browser: built for an accountant, not for someone standing at a counter. There is no "call", no "note that he promised Friday", and access is per Tally user, not per salesperson's customer list.

## 4. Sync Tally to an app built for the field

A connector on the Tally PC sends customers, bills and receipts to an app. This is what [Syncit does](/tally). The difference is not that the number is on the phone — it is what sits around the number:

- Each salesperson sees **only their own customers**.
- The list is ordered by who to talk to **today**: amount × days overdue, with missed promises first.
- One tap to call, to send a polite reminder, to note a promised date, or to record a payment with a photo of the UTR.
- Management sees the whole picture, and which promises were not kept.

## Which should you pick?

If you have under twenty customers on credit, option 1 or 2 is fine. Once more than one person is following up, or you find yourself asking "did anyone call him?", you need the follow-up recorded next to the bill — that is option 4.

Whichever you choose, get bill-wise details right in Tally first. Every method above is only as good as that.
