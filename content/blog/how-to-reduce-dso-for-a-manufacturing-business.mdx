A manufacturer with ₹12 crore of annual credit sales and receivables of ₹2.4 crore is waiting 73 days to be paid. If terms are 45 days, ₹92 lakh of that is simply late — money you are financing, usually on an overdraft at 10 to 12 per cent.

## What DSO is

**Days Sales Outstanding = (Receivables ÷ Credit sales) × Days in the period**

Use credit sales only, and the same period for both numbers. Work yours out in the [DSO calculator](/tools/dso-calculator).

The number to watch is not DSO on its own but **DSO minus your credit terms**. Forty-five-day terms and a 52-day DSO is a healthy business. Thirty-day terms and a 75-day DSO is a problem, even though the second DSO is "only" 23 days higher.

## Seven things that work

### 1. Send the invoice the day goods leave

Many factories raise invoices in a batch at the week-end, or wait for the lorry receipt. Every day between dispatch and invoice is a day added to DSO before the customer has done anything wrong. Dispatch, invoice and LR should reach the customer together, the same day.

### 2. Put the due date on the invoice — as a date

"Payment terms: 45 days" makes the customer do arithmetic. "Due on 21 November 2026" does not.

### 3. Remind before the due date, not after

A short note three days before and on the due date gets more invoices paid on time than any number of calls after. It reads as service. Calls after the date read as chasing. See [reminder messages that work](/blog/polite-payment-reminder-messages-english-hindi).

### 4. Record every promise, with a date

"He said next week" is not a commitment. "₹1.8 lakh on Friday the 14th" is. Write it down where the whole team can see it, and follow up the morning after if it does not arrive. Customers learn quickly which suppliers remember.

### 5. Make it easy to pay

Bank details on every invoice and statement. A UPI or payment link in the reminder. A surprising share of "late" payments are a customer who meant to pay and did not have the account number to hand.

### 6. Check credit before the order, not after dispatch

Set a limit per customer. When a new order would take them over, someone senior decides — before the factory makes it. The conversation "clear the old bill and we will dispatch this one" is far easier before production than after. More in [credit limits](/help/credit-limits).

### 7. Look at ageing every week, by salesperson

Total outstanding hides everything. An ageing report by salesperson shows who has ₹30 lakh sitting past 90 days. Review it every Monday; ask about the same five names until they move. Start from our [ageing template](/tools/outstanding-ageing-template) if you do not have one.

## What not to do

- **Do not stop supply as a first step.** It ends the relationship and often the receivable with it.
- **Do not offer blanket early-payment discounts.** Two per cent for paying 30 days early is an annual rate above 24 per cent. Offer it selectively.
- **Do not let sales be the only ones who know.** If the follow-up lives in one person's head, it leaves with them.

## A realistic target

Bringing DSO to within 7 to 10 days of your terms is achievable in two to three quarters with the steps above. For the ₹12 crore business, that is roughly ₹60 lakh released — without a single new customer.

If your customers are micro or small enterprises' buyers, the [45-day rule under section 43B(h)](/blog/msme-45-day-payment-rule-43bh-for-distributors) gives them their own reason to pay on time.
